# ALPS Foundation Design System

## Product

### Purpose
- Present ALPS Foundation as a credible, contemporary Swiss non-profit for psychedelic science, education, and public dialogue.
- Balance two audiences without over-serving either: researchers, clinicians, and students on one side, and the broader public and supporters on the other.
- Make dense institutional content feel accessible, calm, and intentional.

### Brand Personality
- Precise
- Restrained
- Open-minded

### Emotional Tone
- Quiet confidence over hype
- Intellectual clarity over visual novelty
- Cultural openness without looking whimsical or countercultural by default

## Visual Direction

### Core Aesthetic
- Strict Swiss modernism, softened just enough for a public-facing cultural institution.
- Monochrome first: near-black on near-white, with the spectrum appearing only as a whisper in gradients, not as a brand color.
- Editorial and poster-aware rather than startup-like.

### Anti-Direction
- No cyan-on-dark, neon accents, glow, or tech-conference vibes.
- No generic AI cards, badges, icon blocks, or oversized rounded pills.
- No decorative primary color. The site must stay compatible with the many visual languages represented by partner logos, posters, and association materials.

## Color System

### Base Palette
- `Paper`: warm near-white
- `Paper Alt`: slightly denser off-white for structured surfaces
- `Ink`: soft near-black
- `Ink Soft`: neutral gray for secondary copy
- `Rule`: hairline neutral borders

### Spectrum Usage
- Use full-spectrum gradients only as very subtle atmospheric fills.
- Gradients must feel ambient and diffused, never like a call-to-action accent.
- The homepage hero carries the strongest spectrum treatment.
- Elsewhere, spectrum may appear as soft page wash, soft blur, or faint line treatment.

## Typography

### Font System
- Primary family: Switzer
- Display/headline family: Switzer Black
- Body family: Switzer Variable / standard Switzer weights

### Rules
- Main headings use Switzer Black with tight tracking and compact line-height.
- Supporting headings use strong weight but not full-black unless they are true page anchors.
- Body copy should sit in a consistent reading rhythm with calm leading and modest measure.
- Uppercase labels should be sparse, small, and disciplined.

### Hierarchy
- `H1`: monumental, compact, poster-like
- `H2`: assertive but not theatrical
- `H3`: functional editorial subheads
- Body: readable, steady, slightly softer than headings

## Layout & Rhythm

### Principles
- Favor asymmetry, left alignment, and strong edge relationships.
- Use open space generously; not every section needs a container.
- Prefer separators, alignment, and spacing over boxed cards.
- When surfaces are needed, keep them flat, lightly bordered, and minimally rounded.

### Rhythm
- Use repeated vertical intervals that create a calm editorial cadence.
- Headings should sit close to their copy; sections should breathe more than components.
- Lists of content should often read as rows, stacks, or ruled groups before becoming card grids.

## Components

### Navigation
- Crisp and typographic.
- Use the ALPS wordmark/logo as the identity anchor.
- Actions should look integrated, not like floating pills.

### Buttons
- Sharp geometry.
- Primary buttons are near-black with white text.
- Secondary buttons are outlined or text-based.
- No oversized radius.

### Cards & Panels
- Default stance: avoid them.
- When unavoidable, use thin borders, subtle fills, no decorative shadows, and very small radii.
- Images can create structure without needing a full surrounding card.

### Links
- Text links should feel editorial.
- Underlines and hairline separators are preferred over colored emphasis.

## Motion

### General
- Very smooth, slow, subtle.
- Motion should feel like measured settling, not bouncing or popping.

### Rules
- Prefer opacity and transform.
- Use long easing curves with gentle deceleration.
- Keep reveal distances short.
- Respect reduced motion.

### Usage
- Homepage: quiet load-in sequencing
- Section reveals: soft and restrained
- Hover states: slight opacity, border, or translation shifts only

## Imagery

### Treatment
- Let partner logos, posters, and event photography keep their own identities.
- Frame imagery with neutral structure rather than brand-colored overlays.
- Crop confidently and avoid decorative masks.

## Accessibility

### Requirements
- Strong text contrast in monochrome mode
- Subtle motion must remain readable and optional
- Focus states should be visible without adding loud color
- Typography must remain readable for public audiences, not only design-conscious ones

## Content Style

### Writing Tone
- Clear, direct, evidence-led
- No inflated marketing language
- Short labels, purposeful intros, low redundancy

## Implementation Priorities

1. Establish global tokens for paper, ink, rules, radii, motion, and Switzer display/body roles.
2. Apply Switzer Black to primary headings across the site.
3. Reduce rounding and shadows globally.
4. Replace card-heavy patterns with ruled stacks and flatter surfaces where possible.
5. Use spectrum gradients as background atmosphere only, never as a primary accent system.
